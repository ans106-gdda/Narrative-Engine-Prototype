The study smells of cold ash and older paper. Vesper Ashgrove sits with his back
to the shuttered window, hands folded, a decanter at his elbow that he has not 
touched and does not look at. Somewhere below, Marrow's footsteps make their 
forty-year circuit of the hall.

"One chime gone," Vesper says. "Five remain, and then the sun, and then whatever
is left of this house belongs to somebody. Possibly you." He studies you the way
a man reads a will he has already signed.

Ilsabet Crane stands near the bookcase, weight forward, not quite at rest. 
Tobias Vane has taken the chair nearest the fire and keeps his hands inside his 
sleeves.

"So. Not your virtues — I have no use for those." Vesper's voice stays level, 
courteous, guarded as a shut door. "Tell me what you fear. Precisely. Name the 
thing, not the shape of it."

The fire pops. Nobody offers to answer for you.

"And understand," he adds, "I will remember what you say tonight."
